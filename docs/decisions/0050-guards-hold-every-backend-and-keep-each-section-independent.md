# 0050. The guards hold every backend's draws, and never make a section depend on another's

- **Status:** Accepted. Its same-pitch gap is amended by [ADR 0073](0073-the-gap-is-counted-in-milliseconds-through-the-tempo-map.md), and its rules at a boundary, counted on the score's own notes, by [ADR 0094](0094-an-added-note-changes-no-other-section.md)
- **Date:** 2026-11-09

- `render/guards.py` runs after the song's drifts, on every backend's draws. **Timing**, in turn:
  1. Each offset is cut to ±12 ms (Appendix A), and a grace takes its main note's offset, so they move as one. A grace the score puts on its main's tick, as on the song's first tick where it can't go before it ([ADR 0027](0027-hits-on-one-drum-under-45-ms-apart-are-a-grace-and-its-main.md)), is placed before its main by the gap it was played at, from the source notes.
  2. Across a section boundary, two notes in a row on one pitch each keep to their side: the earlier at most 0.2 of their grid gap late, the later at most 0.2 early, so they keep 0.6 of it whatever the other section draws.
  3. Each note goes to its nearest tick, and to tick 0 when it would be before it. When a grace would start before tick 0, its main goes later with it, so the grace starts at tick 0 and keeps its gap, up to the cap: a gap over 12 ms is shortened to it, since the plan keeps timing within the cap. A main note and its graces keep their order, a tick or more apart.
  4. In time order, a note keeps 0.6 of its grid gap (Appendix A), and at least a tick, from the note before it on its pitch in its own section or played as it came in, by moving later, and from a note after it played as it came in, by moving earlier, with its main note and graces. When it can't keep both, the gap from the note before wins. Next to a kept note the cap wins over the gap: the note stops at ±12 ms, still a tick or more from the kept note, since the plan keeps timing within the cap, and this only happens where the kept note is over 20 ms off its grid point, which already raised an `ambiguous_bars` request.
  5. A note held to its controller events ([ADR 0053](0053-a-controller-bound-note-keeps-its-controller-value.md)) stays inside its window, which wins over both gaps: inside, it keeps its place, and outside, it moves to the nearer edge.
  6. Each note keeps its source length, at least a tick, and ends by the time the next note of its pitch starts, or by the earliest that note could start when it is in another section.
- **Velocity**, in turn:
  1. A draw above 127 is reflected below it, and one below its role's band above it ([ADR 0051](0051-the-tight-style-s-placeholders-fill-in-what-appendix-a-leaves-out.md)), then rounded and moved into its articulation's range ([ADR 0009](0009-m1-resolves-notes-channels-and-velocity-ranges.md)).
  2. Where a section's drum has soft-typed notes, they are held at least 2 below its lowest other note, and the others no lower than 1 below it.
  3. In play order, a note takes neither the velocity of the note before it on its **pitch** nor that of the note before it on its **drum** (family and piece), when that note is in its section or played as it came in, nor that of a note after it played as it came in. The plan says both "per pitch" and "on one drum", so both hold. The nudge is to the nearest velocity that fits, +1 before -1.
  4. A note next to a rendered note of another section, on its pitch or its drum, takes a velocity of a class instead: modulo 4, the bit worth 2 is its place among its pitch's notes modulo 2, and the bit worth 1 its place among its drum's. Neighbours across a boundary always differ in one bit, whatever the other section draws.
- A velocity range too narrow for a rule wins over it, since a velocity outside it plays another articulation. Each guard counts the notes it moved: `gap` those moved later or earlier for their gap, `controller` those their window moved. `unmet` counts what couldn't be held: a range too narrow for a rule, two gaps that can't both be kept, or the cap keeping a note short of a kept note's gap or of its grace's. Such a note also counts under the guard that moved it, but for the cap.
- Notes played as they came in keep their ticks, lengths and velocities, and the guards work around them.
- **Section independence:** no section's draws reach another, so new controls for one section leave every other section's notes byte-identical. Turning `skip` on or off for a section is the one exception: the notes at and just after its boundaries, on the pitches and drums that cross them, then avoid its fixed notes instead of keeping to their class and their side of the gap, a nudge can pass a step or two along a pitch's notes, and when the sections between lack a pitch, the section changed can be two or more away.

**Why:** Guards after the backend hold the plan's limits for the parametric engine and a trained model alike. A rule comparing a note with the section before's draws would change it whenever that section is rendered again, so across a boundary each side keeps a rule of its own, from the score alone.

**Consequences:** A note at a boundary can sit up to 2 velocity steps from its draw, and a note can move up to a fifth of its grid gap less than its draw asked.
