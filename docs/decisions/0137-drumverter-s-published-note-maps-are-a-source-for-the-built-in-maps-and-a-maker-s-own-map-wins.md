# 0137. Drumverter's published note maps are a source for the built-in maps, and a maker's own map wins

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-11-16

- **The source:** drumverter (drumverter.com), a free converter of drum MIDI between drum engines, publishes a note map of each engine it supports, as `engines/<id>.toml` in its source code (github.com/nullcrimson/remidi), 160 at the pinned commit, and as a `.txt` file per engine on its drum maps page for all of them but its own "MidiRemap Standard" layout. The `.txt` files hold the same notes as the 159 engines they map. `tools/drumverter_maps.py` reads the source's `engines/` at one commit, pinned with the archive's SHA-256 in `tools/drumverter_maps.toml` ([ADR 0004](0004-exact-dependency-versions.md)), into the gitignored `build/drumverter/`, and writes each listed library's `data/maps/<library>.toml`. Its `--check` writes nothing and exits 1 when a committed map differs. A map it writes says so in its header, and is changed there, not by hand.
- **The terms:** drumverter's source is under the MIT licence, which lets anyone use, copy, change and publish it, on the condition that its copyright and permission notice go with every copy or substantial portion. That notice is in `data/maps/NOTICE.md`, which ships with the maps in `data/`, on the site too. The site's terms of use add no restriction: the tool is given as it is, with no warranty, its mappings are best effort, and product names are used only to say which layout a map fits.
- **The names:** each of drumverter's names for a note becomes a name of the vocabulary ([ADR 0019](0019-canonical-names-follow-one-grammar.md)):
  - a kick, the snare's strokes, a tom's hit, rimshot and rim, the hats' closed, tight, pedal and pedal splash strokes, a tip stroke as `.tip`, the ride's bow, bell, bell tip and edge (`ride.N.crash`), crashes, chinas, splashes and stacks, and the percussion, each as the vocabulary names it; drumverter's shaker is `maracas.hit`, as [ADR 0135](0135-sleep-token-ii-s-default-kit-and-logic-pro-drums-follow-their-makers-published-maps.md) has Logic's;
  - a cymbal's or ride's "Mute" as its `choke`, the name most built-in maps give a cymbal grabbed after the hit, and a crash's or stack's bow tip as `hit.tip` ([ADR 0062](0062-the-vocabulary-adds-damped-strokes-wires-off-and-crash-tips.md));
  - a hi-hat's controller-shaped notes as `hat.variable`, with a `shape` controller, and drumverter's auxiliary hats as the kit's second and third hats;
  - toms numbered along the kit, rack toms then floor toms, and every family's pieces numbered from 1 in drumverter's order, so a kit with crashes 1 to 4 and 6 has crashes 1 to 5;
  - a hat's loose and open degrees, from the least open, spread over the vocabulary's quarter, half, three-quarter and fully open, the most open being fully open and each at the nearest, as [ADR 0055](0055-m3-adds-maps-for-four-drum-libraries.md) takes Krimh Drums' many degrees;
  - a tip stroke a kit has without its plain stroke as the plain stroke, and a hat with a tight stroke and no closed one with its tight stroke as closed;
  - a note drumverter marks as an engine's main note for a drum is the one written, and the others are only read.
- **Left out, and asked about:** a note whose name the vocabulary has no name for, or which drumverter names unclearly: a hi-hat's bell, a three-quarter-open hat's tip, a crash's bell and bow, a china's or splash's other strokes, a stack's mute, a bell cymbal, the snare's ghost, ruff and side strokes, an alternate kick and unnamed percussion. A stroke whose piece then has no default stroke, such as a choke without its cymbal's hit, is left out too. Each map's header lists what its kits leave out.
- **Confidence:** every entry from drumverter is `source = "mapping"`, at `medium`, since each name is drumverter's reading rather than the maker's. It is `high` only where the library's maker publishes the same note under a name that gives the canonical name directly. `low` stays for names still inferred ([ADR 0025](0025-proposed-names-ship-at-low-confidence.md)). `mapping` thus covers a published third-party mapping as well as a library's own.
- **The maker wins:** where a library's maker publishes its notes, the maker's map is checked against drumverter's note by note. A note the maker names otherwise, and the vocabulary has no name for, is left out, and a built-in map written from a maker's document isn't replaced by drumverter's. Their differences are listed for the owner instead.

**Why:** drumverter names every note of 160 engines, where the inferred maps were guesses from where each note sits, and its licence lets the maps be built from it with its notice. Its notes agree with the maker-based maps on the instrument each note plays, and differ mostly in how finely a stroke is named, so a maker's own map stays ahead of it, and its names are trusted as interpretations, not facts.

**Consequences:** Most built-in kits now report medium rather than low confidence. A change of drumverter's maps reaches the built-in maps only when the pin is moved and the maps are built again, reviewed as any data change. GitHub's archive of a commit is assumed to keep its SHA-256; if it ever changes, the build refuses it.
