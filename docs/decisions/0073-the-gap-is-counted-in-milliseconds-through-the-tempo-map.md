# 0073. The gap is counted in milliseconds through the tempo map, so it never takes a note past the cap

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

- The guards' same-pitch gap ([ADR 0050](0050-guards-hold-every-backend-and-keep-each-section-independent.md)) is counted in milliseconds through the song's tempo map, as the ±12 ms cap is: a note keeps 0.6 of the milliseconds between its grid point and the note before's, measured between their starts, and at least a tick. The gap to a note played as it came in, before or after, is counted the same way.
- A main note and its graces move by one shift in milliseconds, so they keep one offset when a tempo change falls between them. The shift is counted in the ticks of whichever of them has the shortest tick where it is, the main on a tie, so none moves further than it must, and the others go to their nearest tick.
- Between two notes of one section, the gap never takes a note past the cap: the note before is within it, so keeping 0.6 of the gap leaves the later note at most 12 ms less 0.4 of the gap late. A note still goes to a whole tick, and keeps a tick after the note before on its grid point, which can leave it about a tick past the cap for each note before it there. Next to a note played as it came in, the cap still wins over the gap, and `unmet` counts it.
- The gap is held in each pitch's play order: a main note and its graces are taken after the notes before theirs on their pitches, so the gap is always counted from where the note before ends up.
- Each note's side of a boundary is held as the tick it rounds to, and the gap from the note before never pushes a note past it: a latest tick is carried back through the section's notes before it, each by the gap it keeps to the next, which can move a note earlier than its draw, counted under `gap`. Of two notes of one pitch on one grid point either side of a boundary, the later keeps a tick or more after it. All of it comes from the score, so sections stay independent.
- A grace the score puts on its main's tick, as when a note of its pitch played just before it is read there, takes its main later with it to keep its gap from the note before, as far as the cap and the main's bounds allow. Past that the grace goes on alone, its gap to its main is shortened, and `unmet` counts it, as on the song's first tick.
- At one tempo, ticks and milliseconds are in proportion, so a song without a tempo change renders the same bytes as before, but where two notes of one pitch share a grid point or a grace sits on its main's tick and the rules above bind. A tick worked out through milliseconds that is within a millionth of a whole tick is taken as that tick, so float rounding never moves a note by a tick.
- [ADR 0052](0052-each-control-is-tested-against-the-statistic-it-mainly-drives.md)'s properties follow: **Same pitch** also draws up to 120 tempo changes, often at 60 or 600 BPM, so a change is often 10 to 1 either way, and measures the gap in milliseconds, less half a tick where each note lands. **Cap** draws hats on every 16th of the four bars its changes fall in half the time, with such changes between them. Examples pin hits a 16th or a 32nd apart at the cap either way, with a change of 10 to 1 either way between them, on a grid point, or before the first is played, and two notes of one pitch on one grid point across a boundary, before one, and with a grace on the later.

**Why:** Appendix A's timing is in milliseconds, converted with the local tempo, and the cap is the plan's hard limit. Counted in ticks, a tempo change between two notes could make the gap's ticks worth more milliseconds than their grid gap, and take a note far past the cap with nothing counted: hats a 16th apart at 600 BPM, slowing to 60 BPM between them, put the second 19.8 ms late. A unit moved by one shift in ticks did the same to a main note across a tempo change from its grace. Two notes of one pitch read onto one grid point broke the guards at one tempo too: either side of a boundary both were held to the grid point, on one tick; before one, the gap from the first pushed the second past its side, so the next section's note kept under 0.6 of its gap; and a unit taken by its grace's tick could go before a note it follows in play order, so the two overlapped, or, behind one, take its main past the cap.

**Consequences:** Across a tempo change, a note's gap in ticks can differ from 0.6 of its grid gap in ticks, and a grace and its main keep one offset in milliseconds, not one shift in ticks. A note before a boundary can sit earlier than its draw, by what the notes after it in its section need to keep their gaps and the last its side.
